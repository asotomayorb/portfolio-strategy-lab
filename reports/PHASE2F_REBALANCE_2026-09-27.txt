Expanding history
-----------------
The no-rebalancing baselines reproduce the previously observed very large
expanding-history drawdowns (about -89% to -91%). The hybrid overlays materially
reduce those historical drawdowns to roughly -30% to -35%, but also reduce CAGR
substantially. Because the expanding history contains a severe early-history
regime and the common-history is the more directly comparable Phase 2 window,
these are descriptive robustness observations, not a basis for parameter
selection.

Interpretation
--------------
1. In the common history, both hybrid overlays materially reduce CAGR while
   leaving maximum drawdown almost unchanged.
2. The 30% threshold retains more CAGR than 20% for all four candidates, but
   this is a sensitivity observation, not a post-result optimization.
3. Sharpe is modestly higher under the hybrid overlays, while realized turnover
   is substantial: approximately 0.31-0.39 cumulative turnover in the common
   history.
4. S1 and S3 remain practically indistinguishable, consistent with Phase 2E.
5. S2 continues to show a lower common-history drawdown than S1/S3, while S4
   retains a higher CAGR than S1-S3 in the tested baseline and hybrid cases;
   no universal dominance is established.
6. The hybrid rebalancing overlay therefore does not provide evidence for
   replacing the frozen Phase 2 baseline in the recurring-contribution case.
   It remains a possible implementation choice when tighter weight control is
   desired and the investor accepts lower historical growth and more sales.

Important scope
---------------
Phase 2F does not alter or reopen the frozen Phase 2E definitions. It is a
post-Phase-2 implementation sensitivity. No threshold was optimized after
seeing the results.
